# Detailed version

Reader: a student or practitioner who must reproduce, apply and defend the material. Goal: precise understanding and the limits of every claim.

- **Precise definitions.** Name the concept, its symbol, unit or scale and typical range at first use; give the formal definition and say which assumptions it needs.
- **Formulas, rules and procedures in full.** Show the governing equation, rule or algorithm; define every symbol and check units. Derive or justify a result when the derivation explains why it behaves as it does; keep hand calculations in the notebook, not in the markdown or PDF.
- **Assumptions and limits.** State what the model, rule or method ignores and where it stops being valid. Say how the answer changes if an assumption moves.
- **Sources and standards.** Cite the standard, paper, statute or document behind each constant and method (full name at first use), and flag where two sources disagree.
- **Interpret every result with caveats.** Meaning, comparison with a reference value or rule of thumb, sensitivity to the main assumption, practical consequence. Explain surprises (diminishing returns, non-monotonic results, exceptions) and edge cases.
- **Structure.** Task or question → method → result → "Interpretation of ..." per chapter; a summary at the end. Same chapter order and the same figures as the simple version.
- **Introduction pattern in full.** What it is → what it does → analogy → units and values → formula and assumptions → where it breaks → why it matters here.
- **Definitions** go in the chapter of first use (shared rules), in a blockquote box that carries the symbol, unit and formal statement.
