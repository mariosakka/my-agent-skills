# Simplified version

Reader: a newcomer with no background in the subject. Goal: they finish understanding the idea and the result, not the derivation or the fine print.

- **Everyday analogy first.** Explain with something familiar (a fridge, a queue, a blanket, a sponge, a post office). One analogy per concept; do not stack several.
- **Plain words.** Short sentences, one idea per paragraph. Prefer the plain meaning to the technical label, then introduce the technical word once.
- **Minimal symbols and maths.** No derivations; at most one simple formula where it unlocks the result, written in words next to its symbols. Keep units, but explain them in a phrase.
- **Numbers with meaning.** Every number gets its everyday translation ("1.7 degrees lower: roughly 10 to 17 % less heating"). Round sensibly; keep the same figures as the in-depth version.
- **Short.** Say what the step does, what came out, what it means. Skip edge cases, standards detail and caveats beyond the one that changes the conclusion.
- **Structure for skimming.** "Idea", "Recipe" (numbered steps), "What this means" (bullets) when the source has a procedure or calculation; for descriptive material use short sections of "what it is" and "why it matters". Same chapter order as the in-depth version.
- **Definitions** still go in the chapter of first use (shared rules), as a one-line plain meaning plus the analogy, inside a blockquote box.
